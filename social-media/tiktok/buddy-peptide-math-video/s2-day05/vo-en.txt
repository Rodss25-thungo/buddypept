Same vial. Same peptide. Two different clocks. The only difference is temperature. In water, the chemistry that breaks a peptide down is still running: hydrolysis, deamidation, oxidation. Heat speeds that chemistry up. A common pharmaceutical rule of thumb says some reaction rates roughly double for every ten degrees Celsius warmer. It is a rule of thumb, not a universal law. But it explains why cold slows the clock. Room temperature is about twenty-five degrees. A fridge is two to eight. That gap can mean the chemistry clock runs several times slower in the cold. The labels agree. Ozempic's label says a new pen goes in the refrigerator, two to eight degrees, and never in the freezer. Cold slows the chemistry clock. It does not stop it. Day five of ten. Follow so you don't miss day six.
