Light can change an amino-chain, a peptide. But not every chain reacts the same way. It depends on which links it is made of. In lab studies, a few links are the usual suspects: tryptophan, tyrosine, phenylalanine, and cysteine. When light hits them, they can oxidize. A chain without those links has far fewer places for that to happen. But the links do not act alone. Their neighbors matter. In one study, a histidine link next to tryptophan was oxidized under light. The same peptide, with the tryptophan swapped out, did not show it. So the sequence decides. Two chains, same light, different result. That is why some labels say protect from light, and why no single rule covers every chain. Follow for more peptide math.
