Yesterday, the clock started. Today, look closer. Your vial doesn't have one clock. It has two. Clock one: chemistry. In solution, the peptide slowly changes. Reactions like hydrolysis and oxidation never fully stop. Cold slows this clock down. But it never resets. Clock two: microbiology. Every time a vial is opened, contamination becomes possible. Preservatives help suppress bacterial growth. They don't stop the chemistry. Two clocks. Two different risks. One changes the molecule. One contaminates the vial. And here's the trap. The famous twenty-eight days? That number belongs to the microbiology clock. It says nothing about your peptide's chemistry. That's tomorrow. Day two of ten. Both clocks are running. Follow so you don't miss day three.
