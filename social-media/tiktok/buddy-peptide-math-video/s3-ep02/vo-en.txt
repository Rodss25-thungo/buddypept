Say a report lists an amino-chain, a peptide, as 98 percent pure. What does that number mean? A lab separates the sample, and a detector draws peaks. One tall peak is the target chain. The small ones are other things that came along. Purity compares the area of the tall peak to the area of all the peaks. But a peak is a signal, not a weight. Different pieces absorb light differently. In one lab study, tryptophan absorbed about thirty times more than a plain peptide bond at a common wavelength. So area is not mass. Purity says how clean the signal looks. It does not say how much of the vial is chain. Those are two different questions. Follow for more peptide math.
