Say an amino-chain, a peptide, is listed as 98 percent pure (an example number). What does that mean? A lab separates the sample and a detector draws peaks. One tall peak is the target chain. Purity compares the area of that peak to the area of all the peaks. But a peak is a signal, not a weight. In one lab study, tryptophan absorbed about thirty times more than a plain peptide bond at 214 nm (Kuipers & Gruppen, J Agric Food Chem 2007). So area is not mass.
