Bacteriostatic water. It sounds like it protects everything in the vial. It has one job. Here it is. Bacteriostatic water is sterile water with zero point nine percent benzyl alcohol. That benzyl alcohol is a preservative. It helps suppress bacterial growth, which is why it is labeled for multiple doses. Now compare. Sterile water for injection has no preservative. It is meant for a single use. So bacteriostatic water works on the microbiology clock. What it does not do: it does not stop the chemistry clock. It does not make a peptide last longer. And it is not complete protection against contamination either. One job. Suppress bacterial growth. Everything else is still up to the label, the temperature, and the handling. Day four of ten.
