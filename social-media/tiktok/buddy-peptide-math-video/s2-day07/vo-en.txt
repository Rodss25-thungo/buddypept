Shake it or swirl it? The label already answered. Here is why. Inside the vial is the amino-chain, a peptide, folded into a compact shape. When you shake, you create bubbles and foam. Every bubble is a new surface where air meets water. At that surface, some amino-chains can partly unfold. Unfolded chains can stick to each other and form clumps, called aggregates. Labs even shake samples on purpose, to force aggregation in stability testing. A gentle swirl mixes the liquid with almost no new air-water surface. That is why some labels spell it out: swirl the vial gently until fully dissolved. Gentle is not a suggestion. It is part of the method. Day seven of ten. Follow so you don't miss day eight.
